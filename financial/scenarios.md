# Scenarios: bull, base and bear over seven years

Date: 2026-10-17 (revised for the owner decision on segments). Status: draft for owner review. Model: `financial/model.xlsx`, tab Scenarios (all three computed side by side) and every main tab (set Assumptions!B5 to 1, 2 or 3). Year 1 is the first twelve months after the seed closes.

These are plans for a company with no customers yet. On 2026-10-17 the owner decided that scoup.ai is shipper- and importer-first, with carriers as a later, partner-led channel. The model now shows that mix explicitly. Each segment has its own revenue per account: an SME importer follows the investor-pack path ($52K recovered in year 1, 25% share, platform fee from year 2), a mid-market shipper recovers 77% of the importer amount because it has no ocean demurrage and detention, and a carrier pays a flat $7,895 a year (the market-researcher carrier fee) through a channel partner that takes a 20% commission. New direct accounts split 50/50 between shippers and importers.

The three cases share the product, the pricing structure, the segment definitions and the cost lines. They differ only in the scenario inputs below.

| Headcount and spend scale | x 1.10 | x 1.00 | x 0.85 |

In the base case design partners convert, importers add the container-fee and fuel checks and a factoring partner brings carriers from year 3. In the bull case the partner signs in year 2, customers accept the platform fee early and importers extend checks across their whole network. In the bear case recoveries run 20% below plan, buyers insist on long security reviews, the carrier partner slips to year 5, and management freezes hiring for two years.

## Reading the cases

**Base.** Revenue grows from $35K in year 1 to $16.9M in year 7 on 805 accounts: 304 shippers, 301 SME importers and 200 carriers. Importers earn the most per account ($30.8K in year 7), shippers $27.6K, carriers $7.9K, so carriers are a quarter of accounts but 8% of revenue. ARR crosses the $2.5M Series A bar in month 38 and EBITDA turns positive in year 6. The cash low point before the Series A is the end of year 3, at $2.37M with the $7.0M seed. Gross margin starts deeply negative because one claims analyst and the hosting bill arrive before revenue, passes 60% in year 3 and reaches 79% in year 7, close to the 77% Benchmarkit median for B2B software.

**Bull.** Higher recoveries, lower churn and a carrier partner in year 2 bring the milestone forward to month 31 and EBITDA break-even to year 5. The $7.0M seed alone would fund this case to profitability (seven-year need $5.59M including buffer), so the Series A becomes optional growth capital. Tax starts in year 6 once accumulated losses are used up.

**Bear.** Half the direct account wins, 20% lower revenue per account and 30% churn leave ARR at $771K after year 3 and $5.8M after year 7. The two-year hiring hold keeps the company at eleven people in years 3 and 4; when the hold ends, the scaled plan resumes, costs outrun revenue and EBITDA falls to -$5.34M by year 7. This is not a venture business. It shows what the tranche-2 checkpoint protects against: if measured recoveries and acquisition costs look like this at month 22, we stop or pivot with tranche-1 money left. A permanent hold instead reaches break-even in year 6, but only with two claims analysts and two salespeople serving the whole customer base, which we do not consider credible.

Account wins and revenue per account matter about equally: a 20% shortfall in either adds about $0.7M to the seed need, $4.1-4.3M to the seven-year need and two months to the milestone. Acquisition cost matters more. At the sourced pessimistic $31K CAC the plan needs $18.7M of equity and breaks even only in year 7; at the $2.00 benchmark efficiency it needs $38.5M and never breaks even. The all-carrier row is the downside the owner decision removes from the base case: with the same number of accounts, all at carrier economics, year-7 ARR is $6.3M and the business does not work.

## Market size and share of SAM

Market-researcher sized the market for the shipper- and importer-first plan in `research/05-market-size.md` v3 (2026-10-17), which supersedes its earlier $474.2M SAM and $10.30B TAM.

- **SAM: 32,765 accounts, $945.4M a year.** 19,932 mid-market shippers (US Census SUSB 2022 firms with $100M-$999.9M of receipts in manufacturing, wholesale and retail, which at a 4.68% transport-to-sales ratio means $5M-$50M of freight spend) plus 12,833 SME importers (the FMC demurrage and detention pool of $3.08B a year divided by the $240K exposure we model). The dollar figure uses our year-7 segment rates: 19,932 x $27.6K = $550.1M for shippers plus 12,833 x $30.8K = $395.3M for importers. If every importer were also counted as a shipper, the pool would be as low as $550.1M; the overlap is unknown.
- **TAM: 133,256 accounts, $1,855.5M.** The SAM plus 72,053 carriers at $7,895 (5-100 trucks, the later partner channel) and 28,438 brokers at an assumed $12K.

| Share in year 7 | Bull | Base | Bear |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shipper + importer accounts / 32,765 SAM accounts | 847 = 2.6% | 605 = 1.8% | 234 = 0.7% |
| Shipper + importer revenue / $945.4M SAM | 2.6% | 1.6% | 0.5% |
| Shippers / 19,932 | 2.1% | 1.5% | 0.6% |
| SME importers / 12,833 | 3.3% | 2.3% | 0.9% |
| Carriers / 72,053 | 0.5% | 0.3% | 0.1% |
| Shipper revenue / $550.1M shipper SAM | 2.2% | 1.3% | 0.4% |
| Importer revenue / $395.3M importer SAM | 3.3% | 2.1% | 0.6% |

The 12,833 importers are sized at about $36K of D&D recovery each, the mid-size importers our revenue path assumes, so the earlier concern (importer revenue at 44% of a smaller importer pool) no longer applies. A 1.8% share of SAM accounts after seven years is an ambitious but not implausible plan for a new vendor, and the market does not cap any case. The shipper count rests on a 2008 transport-to-sales survey; at 2% or 10% instead of 4.68% the shipper count is 9,366 or 35,349 (research sensitivity), which would put base year-7 shippers at 3.2% or 0.9% of their segment. Market-researcher also estimates an average mid-market shipper with $9.53M of freight spend recovers $95.3K a year at a 1% recoverable rate (assumed), inside our recovery path of $85K-$115K, which supports the shipper revenue per account.

## Open questions

1. **Shipper count basis.** The 19,932 mid-market shippers depend on a 2008 transport-to-sales ratio (4.68%); a current figure would firm up the SAM.
2. **Shipper and importer overlap.** Some firms are both; the SAM could be as low as $550.1M (shippers only) because the overlap is unknown.
3. **Importer size.** The 12,833 importers are the FMC-pool cap and sit inside a public bound of 4,160-20,279 importers with roughly $17M-$169M of annual imports (Census 2024 importer counts, BTS and S&P container data, per market-researcher). A 2,000-TEU importer (about $67.5M of imports) is closer to a Census large (500+ employees) importer than a typical SME; an exact count needs bill-of-lading data.
4. **Competitor pricing.** None published; fee path stays assumed until pilots test it.
5. **Shipper recovery.** The 0.77 multiplier rests on an assumed $24K of accessorial and detention audit recovery per shipper.
6. **Bear-case response** (two-year hold, permanent hold or stop) is an owner decision.
7. **Entity.** Whether US staff sit in the Norwegian AS or a Delaware subsidiary affects payroll taxes and the 22% tax rate.

# 01 Competitors

Status: pre-product. The product, scoup.ai, runs on synthetic data only, has never been deployed and has no paying customers. Every "why we can beat them" line below is a hypothesis about positioning, not a measured result.

Research date: 2026-10-17. All URLs were accessed on that date unless an entry says otherwise. Where a vendor page was rendered by a fetch tool rather than read as raw HTML, the figure is marked "(vendor page)" and should be treated as the vendor's own claim. Pricing is "not public" wherever we found no published price; we did not estimate any.

## How we chose the set

In one workflow, scoup.ai does five things: it assembles detention and accessorial evidence packets and demand letters for trucking, audits ocean demurrage and detention (D&D) invoices, scores carrier charges, gives a fuel-surcharge outlook, and routes every outgoing claim through a four-eyes approval step. No single competitor covers that combination, so the set has three groups. Six are freight audit and payment (FAP) firms that a mid-market shipper would compare us against: Loop, Cass, Trax, CTSI-Global, nVision Global and Intelligent Audit. One is an ocean D&D specialist that audits and disputes container fees: BlueCargo. Two are carrier-side detention tools that track dwell time from ELD geofences and create claims: Hey Bubba and FleetOptos.

We checked the names suggested in the brief. Cass, Trax, CTSI-Global, nVision Global and Loop are real and relevant. Portcast is real, but its public material describes a container visibility and ETA platform with a D&D invoice module, and we could not confirm pricing, customers or dispute volumes, so it sits in the adjacent list rather than the core set. Kpler is a commodity and maritime data company; we found no evidence it sells D&D invoice auditing, so we dropped it. "Fenix Parcel" could not be matched to a verifiable company; parcel refund auditors are outside our wedge in any case.

The market-analysis file in the main checkout says its competitor descriptions were "characterizations from general knowledge" and not verified. This file replaces those characterizations with sourced entries. Two repo statements changed on re-verification: Loop's funding is now known ($160M total, where the repo said "not verified"), and Intelligent Audit charges a subscription, not audit fees (its own FAQ says so).

## Forwarders and customs brokers as D&D substitutes (added 2026-10-17)

An SME importer’s first stop for a D&D dispute is usually its forwarder or customs broker, so we looked for priced D&D recovery services from them. We found dispute intake and guidance, not a recovery product. CastleGate Forwarding states that D&D disputes "(or requests for fee mitigation, refund or waiver) for U.S. shipments must be raised by entering a ticket through our website in Partner Home". A search summary describes a DHL Global Forwarding "Disputes Resolution Team" for D&D mitigation, refunds and waivers; the DHL PDF did not return readable text, so this is unconfirmed. Mohawk Global, a customs broker, publishes a guide on how to dispute D&D charges.
Source: CastleGate Forwarding, Regulatory, https://castlegateforwarding.com/resources/regulatory, accessed 2026-10-17
Source: DHL Global Forwarding, FMC D&D mitigation (PDF), https://dhl.com/content/dam/dhl/local/us/dhl-global-forwarding/documents/pdf/us-dgf-fmc-dds-mitigation.pdf, attempted 2026-10-17, text not readable
Source: Mohawk Global, "How to dispute detention and demurrage charges", https://mohawkglobal.com/trade-translation/how-to-dispute-detention-and-demurrage-charges/, accessed 2026-10-17

Two points follow. First, when the forwarder acts as an NVOCC it is itself the billing party, so its dispute desk handles complaints against its own invoices; an independent checker has no such conflict. Second, forwarders and customs brokers already hold the importer relationship and the documents, which makes them a possible channel as much as a substitute. We found no forwarder or broker publishing a fee for D&D recovery; that is a search result, not proof that none exists.

## What the set tells us

The pattern holds across the shipper-side firms we could read. They audit invoices against contract rates, pay carriers and report spend. Their pages say little or nothing about building a counterparty-ready dispute for a single accessorial, and none of them sells to carriers. The carrier-side tools capture geofence time and stop at the claim line. BlueCargo is the only firm in the set that markets evidence-backed dispute resolution with a stated success rate, and it does so only for port D&D.

That leaves the gap scoup.ai was designed for: one evidence, deadline and approval workflow that works both for the party owed the money and for the party asked to pay it. The gap is visible on paper. Whether buyers will pay for it is what the design-partner phase has to measure, and the two firms most able to close it from above are Loop (capital) and Intelligent Audit (already moving down-market).

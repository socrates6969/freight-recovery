# Freight Recovery - SEO, Content and Digital Marketing Plan (B2B)

> **Status: pre-product plan. Nothing here has been run or measured.** There is no live website, no traffic, no rankings, no leads and no customers. This document contains **no traffic, ranking, conversion or revenue figures**, and none should be added until they come from a real analytics or search-console export. Keyword "volumes" are deliberately left blank (`TBD: measure`) because we have no tool access to verify them. Competitor characterizations come from `business/market-analysis.md` and are **unverified**; check them before any public comparison page.
>
> Audience for this plan: the founder/operator running go-to-market. Buyers: freight-intensive mid-market shippers (assumed ~$5M-$50M annual freight spend) and small/mid-size carriers (assumed 5-100 trucks). See `business/business-plan.md`.

## 0. What this plan is, and what must happen first

1. **This is a plan plus on-page foundations**, not an audit. Section 9 and `marketing/landing/` give the structure and copy outline to build the first pages correctly.
2. **A real SEO audit requires a live site.** A FITq-style audit (or any crawl/Core Web Vitals/indexation/schema/AI-visibility audit) measures a deployed, crawlable URL. Until a site is live there is nothing to crawl, no Search Console property, no index coverage, no field performance data, and no AI-answer presence to test. After launch, run the audit at: week 0 (baseline), after the first 10 pages ship, and then quarterly. Do not fabricate a baseline.
3. **SEO is not the first growth lever.** The business plan's go-to-market starts with design-partner outreach (months 0-4), because the product is unvalidated. SEO and content are a slow, compounding channel that (a) supports credibility when a design partner Googles us, and (b) builds an asset that works while the founder sells. Do the cheap foundations early; do not spend heavily on content volume before a partner has confirmed leakage is real.
4. **Precondition for any claim on the site:** every number on a page is either cited to a primary source with a grade (A/B/C as in the business plan) or labeled as an assumption/illustration. No testimonials, logos, "customers", savings percentages or "AI-powered" superlatives that we cannot substantiate. Marketing claims about savings are a legal/trust risk (US FTC substantiation expectations; Norway/EU consumer and marketing law apply to B2B misrepresentation too). Get counsel review before launch.

## 1. Positioning for search (what we can honestly say)

Wedge (from the plan): an agent-assisted workflow that assembles the **detention / accessorial evidence packet** and a DRAFT demand, with a human approving everything, for mid-market shippers and small carriers the large freight-audit firms under-serve. Contingency pricing is the intended pilot model.

Sayable today: "We are building a tool that turns rate confirmation + BOL + invoice into a documented, auditable recovery claim; we are looking for design partners." Not sayable today: any recovery rate, any dollar saved, any accuracy figure, "trusted by", "SOC 2", "integrates with <TMS/ELD>" (no integration exists).

## 4. Lead magnet: a reproducible savings / recovery calculator

**Goal:** a free tool a buyer can use in two minutes that produces a *transparent estimate and the arithmetic*, then offers a no-cost leakage review on their own closed files (the design-partner motion). It must be honest by construction.

Design rules:
- **The user supplies the inputs; we supply no hidden stats.** Inputs (shipper): annual freight spend, share of spend that is accessorial (user's own number or "I don't know" -> prompt to use their last 3 months of invoices), number of loads/yr. Inputs (carrier): loads/yr, share of stops with waiting beyond free time, average hours over free time, detention rate per hour (from their rate cons), % of detention currently billed and % of billed that gets paid.
- **Formula is shown and editable.** Carrier example: `unbilled_or_unpaid = loads x stop_share x avg_hours_over_free x rate x (1 - paid_through_share)`; shipper example: `exposure = spend x accessorial_share x unsupported_share`. Every line of the arithmetic is printed back, in the spirit of the product's own calculation trails.
- **Defaults are labeled assumptions with ranges, not findings.** Pre-fill nothing that looks like a statistic. If a default is offered to help a user start (e.g., a 2-hour free time, which is a common contract term but varies), label it "typical contract term; check your rate confirmation". Industry-wide "unrecovered %" numbers are grade C and must not be pre-filled.
- **Output is a range and a caveat, never a promise.** "If your inputs are right, this is the order of magnitude of detention you may not be collecting before fees. It is not a quote or a guarantee. Fees and winnable share depend on your contracts and evidence."
- **Reproducibility:** publish the formula and a fixed worked example (hypothetical numbers, labeled "illustration") so anyone can recompute it by hand; ship the calculator as a small static page with the logic in plain JavaScript and a unit test of the worked example. Same inputs always give the same output; no hidden scoring, no "AI estimate".
- **Privacy:** compute client-side; do not transmit inputs unless the user submits the lead form. State what is stored, by whom, and for how long. GDPR/ePrivacy consent for any email capture (the operator is in Norway); no pre-ticked boxes; a working unsubscribe.
- **Follow-up offer:** "Send us 20-50 closed loads (read-only, under NDA) and we will run the same arithmetic on your real documents." That is the design-partner pitch and the first honest data source.
- Spec and copy: `marketing/landing/calculator-spec.md`.

Other lead magnets (same honesty rules): a **detention evidence checklist** (what proof wins a detention claim: rate-con terms, appointment, arrival/departure stamps, contact log), a **rate-confirmation red-flag checklist** (accessorial clauses to negotiate), and a **sample evidence packet and DRAFT demand letter built on synthetic data** (we already generate these in the repo's fixtures; label as synthetic).

## 5. Content plan

Principles: evidence-based; every explainer has a worked example with arithmetic; cite primary sources; written by a named person with domain review (the plan notes no freight-audit expert is on the team yet, so **get a domain expert to review before publishing** and say who they are only with their consent); no AI-sloppy filler; date-stamp and maintain.

Pillars:
1. **Detention and accessorial fundamentals** (glossary + explainers; Cluster C). Build first; cheapest, most reusable.
2. **How to win a claim** (evidence, timelines, contract language; the evidence-packet concept).
3. **Shipper-side audit** (unauthorized and unsupported accessorials; rate-con negotiation).
4. **Build in public, honestly**: pre-product progress, what we got wrong, the evaluation method. Credible for a founder-led B2B start; no fabricated metrics.
5. **Data and benchmarks** (section 7) once real, consented data exists.

Cadence is a capacity decision, not a target: start with ~6-8 foundation pages (glossary hub, detention calculation explainer, dispute playbook for each side, evidence checklist, calculator, one neutral comparison guide) before any blog cadence. Re-evaluate from Search Console after launch.

## 7. Public benchmark (the earned-media asset, deferred until the data is real)

Concept: a recurring, methodology-first **"Detention and accessorial recovery benchmark"** for mid-market shippers and small carriers: how often detention is billed, how often it is paid, how long recovery takes, what evidence correlated with payment.

Rules so it is credible rather than marketing:
1. **It does not exist until real, consented, anonymized data does** (post-pilot, G3+). Publishing before that would be invented data. Until then, the page can describe the *methodology* and invite participation, with no numbers.
2. **Methodology published up front:** definitions, sample size and selection, time window, exclusions, how anonymization and counterparty de-identification work, known biases (design partners self-select), and confidence limits. Release the aggregated tables and the code that computes them.
3. **Consent and contracts:** written permission from every data contributor; no counterparty (carrier/shipper) identifiable; legal review of data-sharing terms and competition-law sensitivity before releasing price-related aggregates.
4. **Distinguish measured from vendor-grade figures** (the repo's A/B/C grading). Prefer ATRI as the third-party anchor and say plainly what it does and does not measure (cost of waiting, not recoverable dollars).
5. Re-run on a fixed cadence only if the sample supports it; if not, say so.

## 9. On-page SEO checklist (apply to every page; verified post-launch by an audit)

**Intent and content**
- [ ] One primary query/intent per page; the page answers it better than the top results (worked example, original table, checklist).
- [ ] H1 states the topic plainly; H2/H3 follow the questions a buyer asks. One H1.
- [ ] Definition or direct answer in the first ~100 words (snippet/AI-answer friendly).
- [ ] Every number is cited (primary source + grade) or labeled assumption/illustration; dates on time-sensitive claims.
- [ ] Pre-product disclosure visible where claims about capability appear; no unsubstantiated superlatives.
- [ ] Named author, reviewer credential (with consent), published and updated dates.
- [ ] Clear next step (CTA): calculator, checklist, or "book a design-partner conversation".

**Metadata and structure**
- [ ] Unique `<title>` (~50-60 chars, primary phrase early) and meta description (~120-155 chars, benefit + CTA, no stuffing).
- [ ] Clean, short, lowercase, hyphenated URL; canonical tag; no duplicate parameter URLs.
- [ ] Open Graph and Twitter card tags; descriptive social image with alt text.
- [ ] Descriptive alt text on informative images; decorative images empty alt; no text baked into images.
- [ ] Internal links: every page reachable in <=3 clicks; descriptive anchors; hub-and-spoke between glossary, guides, calculator and solution pages.
- [ ] Breadcrumbs on deep pages.

**Structured data (only for what is true and visible on the page)**
- [ ] `Organization` (name, URL, logo, contact; sameAs only for real profiles), `WebSite`, `BreadcrumbList`.
- [ ] `Article`/`BlogPosting` for guides (author, dates), `FAQPage` only where an on-page FAQ exists, `SoftwareApplication` only once a product page describes real, available software. **No fabricated `Review`/`AggregateRating`.**
- [ ] Validate with Google's Rich Results Test / Schema validator after launch.

**Technical**
- [ ] HTTPS, HSTS, one canonical host (www/non-www), 301 redirects only, no redirect chains.
- [ ] `robots.txt` and XML sitemap with correct lastmod; sitemap submitted in Search Console. Block staging environments and the product's authenticated app/API from indexing (`noindex`/auth); never index customer data or the API docs behind keys.
- [ ] Core Web Vitals within Google's "good" thresholds measured on **field** data after launch (LCP, INP, CLS); lightweight pages, optimized images, no layout shift from the calculator.
- [ ] Mobile-first layout, readable fonts, tap targets; accessible (WCAG 2.2 AA): headings, contrast, keyboard navigation, form labels (also an SEO and a legal-risk item).
- [ ] Server-rendered/static HTML for content (the calculator can be client-side, but the explanatory text must be in the HTML).
- [ ] Custom 404 with search/links; no soft 404s.
- [ ] Hreflang if/when a Norwegian version is published (the repo already keeps `*.no.md` docs); do not auto-translate without review.

**Trust and compliance**
- [ ] Privacy policy, cookie/consent banner appropriate for EU/EEA visitors, working unsubscribe, imprint/company details.
- [ ] Security page stating only what is true today (see the repo's README "Before real customer data"): no "SOC 2", "enterprise-grade" or certifications we do not hold.
- [ ] Comparison pages: facts verified and date-stamped; no misleading use of competitors' marks; fair where rivals are stronger.

**Launch and audit gates**
- [ ] Search Console and analytics verified; sitemap submitted; staging blocked.
- [ ] **Then** run a real SEO audit (FITq-style or equivalent: crawl, indexation, Core Web Vitals, schema, internal links, AI visibility) on the live URL and record the baseline. Re-audit after the first 10 pages and quarterly.

## 10. Sequencing

1. **Now (no site):** decide the domain; draft the landing page (`marketing/landing/`), calculator spec and the foundation pages; line up a domain-expert reviewer; keep the design-partner outreach going (that is the real go-to-market). Get legal review of claims language.
2. **Launch:** publish the small foundation set, verify Search Console/analytics, submit the sitemap, run the first live audit (baseline).
3. **After first partner conversations:** adjust copy to the language buyers actually use; add the neutral comparison guide; start LinkedIn cadence from real learnings.
4. **After pilots (G3+):** with consent and legal review, publish the first benchmark and case study; consider test-sized paid search.

## 11. Risks and honest limits

- **Pre-product credibility gap:** an unvalidated product with polished marketing erodes trust. Mitigate with explicit status, worked examples, and a design-partner offer instead of claims.
- **Small niche keyword volumes:** the market is a few thousand accounts; relevance beats reach. Volumes are unmeasured here.
- **Content moat is thin:** guides are copyable; the durable asset is proprietary dispute-outcome data and the benchmark, which exist only after real volume.
- **Legal/regulatory:** advertising substantiation, data-protection, outreach rules, and competition-law sensitivity on price benchmarks need counsel.
- **Dependence on one operator:** founder time is the constraint; prefer fewer, better assets.

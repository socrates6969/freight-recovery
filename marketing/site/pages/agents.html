The agent team

Six agents, one invoice, every line

The team checks carrier freight invoices against the shipper's contract and tariff and flags overcharges. Deterministic code does all money math; language-model agents only do judgment: reading free text, mapping an unfamiliar charge name, a stricter second opinion and the wording of the claim letter. The deterministic path runs complete without any model.

Status, plainly: the browser app runs the core checks, the evidence packet and the DRAFT letter today, on your device, from figures you enter. The six-agent team is built and unit-tested in the repository but not yet connected to the app or the team workspace. Its test figures below are on synthetic invoices, not real ones.

01

Intake

Built, not in the app yet

Turns a carrier invoice into one normalized invoice, whatever form it arrives in.

Input

An EDI 210 invoice (a subset of segments), a flat CSV, pre-extracted fields, or free text.

Output

A normalized invoice: carrier, PRO, bill of lading, dates, lines, weights and totals.

  • Parsers handle EDI 210, CSV and extracted fields with no model at all.
  • A model is used only for free text no parser accepts; its answer must fit the schema and is marked "confirm the fields".
  • Today in the browser app you type or paste the figures, or import a CSV for an ocean D&D invoice.
02

Contract and tariff

Built, not in the app yet

Finds the contract and lane that govern the invoice and the terms that apply to each charge.

Input

The normalized invoice and the shipper's contracts, tariffs, fuel tables and diesel index.

Output

The applied terms: lane rate, fuel, accessorial schedule, detention, reweigh rule and claim deadline.

  • Contract by carrier and effective dates; lane by origin, destination and mode.
  • Claim deadline: the invoice date plus the contract's claim window.
  • A model may map a charge name that matches no schedule entry, but may answer only a code from the schedule, or nothing.
  • Today in the browser app: the rate confirmation terms you enter, public carrier tariff presets for ocean D&D, and Recall's 256 public tariff charge names for unfamiliar charges.

04

Evidence

Live in the browser app

Attaches the proof to every finding: the contract clause, the invoice line, the bill of lading and any prior invoice.

  • In the app today: an evidence packet (Markdown) and a findings file (JSON) with every calculation, built on your device.
  • In the agent team: a clause and document trail per finding, which the reviewer checks.
05

Reviewer

Built, not in the app yet

Independently re-derives every finding and returns confirmed, needs review or rejected.

  • Billed minus expected equals the amount in dispute; the cited lines and clauses exist; the rule's preconditions still hold.
  • No line is claimed twice, and a finding past its claim deadline is rejected.
  • Review-only and low-confidence findings are never confirmed automatically.
  • A model second opinion can only make a verdict stricter, never looser.
  • In the app today: confirmed findings are kept apart from items that need a person, and only confirmed ones are totalled.
06

Claim

Live in the browser app

Drafts the dispute from confirmed findings only, listing billed, expected and basis for each, the total and the deadline.

  • Every draft carries the banner: DRAFT, for human review, not sent, not legal advice.
  • A model may word the opening paragraph; it is thrown away if it contains any digit, so no model text can state an amount.
  • In the app today: the DRAFT letter built from your confirmed findings, downloaded on your device.

From finding to money

How the amount in dispute is collected

  1. Load the paperwork. Carrier invoices, the contracts and tariffs, and the shipment evidence: bills of lading, gate times, approvals and prior invoices.
  2. Flag and confirm. The team flags overcharges. Only findings the reviewer confirms go into the claim; review items are listed separately and not claimed until a person accepts them.
  3. A person files. Someone approves the draft and files it with the carrier before the contract claim deadline, which is computed per contract.
  4. The carrier pays. By credit memo, refund or a short-pay deduction on the next payment. Each line carries billed, expected, the clause and the documents a carrier needs to accept it.

Nothing in the agent code sends a claim, moves money or contacts a carrier.

Synthetic test, not real invoices

What the team does on synthetic invoices

135 invoices from invented carriers, contracts, lanes and diesel prices, generated by the same code base (seed 20261022): 45 clean and 90 with one injected overcharge each. Nine kinds, ten of each: duplicate invoice, fuel on the wrong base, unauthorized detention, reweigh without evidence, rate above contract, arithmetic error, fuel at the wrong diesel week, unauthorized accessorial and wrong freight class. The clean set includes hard cases such as a reweigh with a certificate and detention billed correctly.

  • 0 of 45

    clean invoices with a claimed finding

  • 90 / 90

    injected overcharges found, every kind 10 of 10, each amount equal to the injected dollars to the cent

  • 5 of 45

    clean invoices sent to human review (two stop-offs on identical lines, $75 each), not claimed

What these numbers do not show: the same team wrote the generator and the checks, so the injected errors match the rules' definitions and perfect scores are expected, not impressive. The set does not test PDF or OCR extraction, real contract wording and exceptions, carrier-specific charge codes, multi-stop shipments, corrected rebills or the model agents, which are tested only with scripted answers. Real-world results are unknown until the team runs on an early customer's closed audit files with known outcomes.

Rules the agents keep

  • Code does all money math

    No model output is used as money. Amounts come from versioned, readable rules with exact decimal arithmetic.

  • Confirmed or not claimed

    Only findings the reviewer confirms reach the claim; everything else waits for a person.

  • Stricter, never looser

    A model second opinion can reject or downgrade a finding, never raise one.

  • Untrusted documents

    Invoice and document text is fenced as untrusted data in every model prompt.

  • A person approves

    Nothing is sent, short-paid or disputed by the software, and no money moves through it.

  • Measured, and labelled

    Every published figure says what it was measured on; synthetic results are never presented as real-world results.

Open gaps

  • The agent team is not yet connected to the API or the app; contracts and lanes have no storage or editor yet.
  • EDI 210 support is a subset; EDI 211 and 214 and carrier-specific charge codes are not mapped.
  • Lane matching is exact on city and state; zip- and zone-based tariffs are not implemented.
  • The model agents have no repeated-run evaluation and no live-model test yet.

Ocean D&D and the FMC rule

The browser app's ocean audit applies the content and timing provisions of 46 CFR Part 541. Requirements and deadlines depend on the billing party and the facts; confirm current rules and any dispute with counsel or your customs broker.

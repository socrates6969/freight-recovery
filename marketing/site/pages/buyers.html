Shippers: take accessorial review off AP's desk

Every accessorial, detention and fuel line on a carrier or broker invoice is checked against the rate confirmation that should have authorized it, with the source attached. Accessorial and detention lines arrive with little to check them against, so someone in AP has to find the rate confirmation, match each line and chase the ones that do not fit. In a survey by RXO, a freight broker, 61% of shippers agreed that managing accessorial requests is one of the biggest time-wasters for their accounting team, and 70% said carriers are usually honest when requesting accessorials. So the job is mostly checking, quickly and with the evidence kept.

  • Accessorial charges: each line checked against the rate confirmation, with charge names recognised across carriers' different wording.
  • Detention billed to you: waiting time recomputed from the contract terms and time records.
  • Fuel surcharges: recomputed from the official weekly diesel price and your own surcharge table, so a wrong week shows up.

Ambiguous lines go to a person; nothing is disputed automatically.

Source: RXO (a freight broker; survey of 750 shippers and carriers), "What Shippers & Carriers Really Think About Trucking Accessorials (Original Research)", 25 November 2024, https://rxo.com/resources/research/freight-accessorials/, accessed 2026-10-17.

Importers: check every D&D invoice before the 30-day window closes

We check each container detention and demurrage invoice against the FMC billing rule's content and timing requirements and the carrier's tariff, and list what to dispute and by when. Under the rule (46 CFR Part 541), a billing party's failure to meet the invoice content and timing requirements relieves the billed party of the obligation to pay, and the billed party has 30 calendar days from the invoice date to request mitigation, refund or waiver. In September 2025 the D.C. Circuit set aside one provision, 46 CFR 541.4; the rest of the rule remains in effect. We describe what the rule requires and never tell you that a specific charge is not owed: confirm with counsel or your customs broker.

If your forwarder handles D&D for you, the review still helps: many forwarders pass the charges through, and the list shows what to dispute with them or through them.

Sources: Thompson Hine, "FMC Issues Final Rule on Billing Requirements and Practices for Detention and Demurrage Invoices", https://www.thompsonhine.com/insights/fmc-issues-final-rule-on-billing-requirements-and-practices-for-detention-and-demurrage-invoices/, accessed 2026-10-17; Thompson Hine, "Court sets aside part of FMC's demurrage and detention billing rule", 26 September 2025, https://www.thompsonhine.com/insights/court-sets-aside-part-of-fmcs-demurrage-and-detention-billing-rule/, accessed 2026-10-17.

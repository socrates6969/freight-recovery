The platform, module by module

What the prototype does today, how each part is tested, and where its limits are. It is built for shippers and importers checking the invoices they pay; the same engine can build a carrier's detention packet. Everything below runs in our own test environment on synthetic data and published reference data.

Status: prototype built and exercised in internal tests. The website is public; the software is not a hosted customer service. No customer has used it, and no customer data has been processed.

Invoice lines and evidence

For each load the rules compare the invoice with the rate confirmation and the time records: when the detention clock starts, free time, rounding, the hourly rate and cap, and accessorial lines the rate confirmation never authorised. Amounts are computed with exact decimal arithmetic by explicit rules. Machine learning is used only to read and classify messy inputs, never to decide an amount.

Findings are split into confirmed (they count toward a short-pay or dispute) and needs review (listed separately, never disputed automatically). The letter is always a DRAFT until a second person approves it.

Charge intelligence

Carriers name the same charge in many ways, and a charge that is not recognised is never checked against the contract. The classifier compares a new charge name with labelled examples and suggests a category only when it is confident; otherwise it asks a reviewer. Each confirmation or correction is stored for that customer and used on the next invoice. There is no model retraining and no data is shared between customers.

The test above uses charge names from public tariffs, not invoice lines from customers. A gate in our release pipeline stops a release if those results fall.

Fuel surcharges and the cost outlook

A fuel surcharge is meant to follow the weekly diesel price published by the US Energy Information Administration. The platform recomputes it from that series and the customer's own table, so an invoice that used the wrong week is flagged with the difference.

The cost outlook is experimental. Although internal backtests compare forecasts with a no-change baseline, the data preparation, evaluation window and results have not been independently reproduced, so no performance claim is made. Treat any output as informational only.

Ocean container fees

46 CFR Part 541 includes invoice-content and timing provisions and a process for requesting mitigation, refund or waiver. Section 541.4 is currently reserved following a court decision; the remaining provisions include deadlines that vary by billing-party type. This prototype can surface dates and required fields for human review, but it does not decide whether a charge is legally owed or whether a specific invoice complies. Confirm current rules and any dispute with counsel or your customs broker.

Approval and audit trail

  • Every action is written to an append-only, hash-linked audit log, so changes to evidence or approvals can be detected.
  • The approval rules are enforced twice: in the application and by database triggers.
  • Learning components never approve anything. Every finding they influence is marked, and the labels that influenced it are recorded.

What is not here yet

  • No hosted service and no customer deployments.
  • No TMS, ELD, ERP, carrier-portal or accounting integrations.
  • No measured results on real invoices; those come from design partners.
  • No external security assessment yet. See security status.

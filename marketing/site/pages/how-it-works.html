How accessorial and D&D invoice review works

The pipeline, described as it actually behaves today, from the side of the shipper or importer paying the invoice. Pre-product; examples below are synthetic.

1. Ingest

You provide one shipment's documents (PDF, CSV or text): the carrier or broker invoice, the rate confirmation and the BOL or gate record; for ocean fees, the D&D invoice and the carrier's tariff. Each file is fingerprinted with SHA-256 so the packet can later show exactly which files the numbers came from.

2. Apply the rules

Rules compare the invoice with the rate confirmation and the gate record: detention clock start (for example, early arrival starts the clock at the appointment), free time, rounding, hourly rate and cap, and accessorial lines that the rate confirmation never authorized. Each finding lists its calculation steps so a person can check it by hand.

Illustration on synthetic data: a detention line on an invoice. Free time 2 h, rate $60/h, cap $480. Appointment 08:00, departure 13:10 gives 5 h 10 min on site; 3 h 10 min beyond free time, rounded per contract rule, at $60/h. The packet prints each of those steps and the contract rule that was applied, so a line billed for more than the terms support shows the difference. Real contracts vary, and the rules are illustrative until validated on real invoices.

3. Review, then decide

  • Confirmed findings count toward the short-pay or dispute.
  • Needs human review findings (low confidence, or possibly legitimate with a receipt) are listed separately and are not disputed.
  • The short-pay or dispute letter is generated as a DRAFT, not sent, not legal advice. A person approves and sends it.

4. Approve with a second person

Nothing leaves the platform until someone other than the author approves it. People who edited the source data, or whose charge corrections influenced the result, cannot approve it either. Every step is recorded in a tamper-evident audit log.

The same flow for fuel and ocean fees

  • Fuel surcharges: the platform recomputes the surcharge from the official weekly diesel price and the customer's own table, and shows which week the invoice used.
  • Ocean container fees: each invoice is compared with content and timing provisions in 46 CFR Part 541 and the carrier tariff, with relevant dates surfaced for human review. Section 541.8 provides the billed party at least 30 calendar days from invoice issuance to request mitigation, refund or waiver; deadlines and requirements vary by billing-party type. Section 541.4 is currently reserved following a court decision. The draft letter quotes the rule and is not sent automatically. The software does not decide whether a charge is owed; confirm current rules and any dispute with counsel or your customs broker.

Sources: Electronic Code of Federal Regulations, 46 CFR Part 541, current through 2026-10-15, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-46/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-541; Federal Maritime Commission, "U.S. Court of Appeals Issues Decision in Case on Demurrage and Detention Billing Practices", https://www.fmc.gov/articles/u-s-court-of-appeals-issues-decision-in-case-on-demurrage-and-detention-billing-practices/, accessed 2026-10-19.

Summary

  • The problem. Mid-size US importers pay container demurrage and detention (D&D) invoices they have a right to dispute, and mid-market shippers spend accounting hours checking truck accessorial charges by hand. Since 28 May 2024 the FMC billing rule has said that a D&D invoice failing its content and timing requirements relieves the billed party of the obligation to pay, with 30 calendar days to dispute. (Business plan, section 1.)
  • What is built. Software that turns an invoice and the documents behind it into a cited packet: each finding shows its arithmetic and points at the document line it rests on, and nothing is ready to send until a second person who did not prepare it has approved it. The ocean D&D audit is the best-evidenced part; the shipper audit checks accessorial, fuel-surcharge and linehaul lines against the shipper's own rate confirmation and fuel table. It does not yet send anything, record what a counterparty paid, or read free-layout documents. (Business plan, sections 1 and 3.)
  • Who buys. Mid-size importers and mid-market shippers with $5M to $50M of freight spend; many companies are both. Carriers come later, through factoring and broker partners. (Business plan, section 1; owner decision of 2026-10-17.)
  • Market. Our research sizes the serviceable market at $945.4M a year across 32,765 accounts. That is a model built from public data, not a measurement of demand. (Business plan, section 6; market research, document 04.)
  • Competition. This side of the market is contested: Loop, Intelligent Audit and BlueCargo all sell into it. We compete where the buyer wants no fee until money is found, no integration for the pilot, and a line-by-line reason before anything is short-paid. (Business plan, sections 4 and 7.)

We describe what the FMC rule requires; whether a specific charge is owed is a legal judgement, so confirm with counsel or your customs broker.

Sources: scoup.ai business plan (document 01), section 1 "Executive summary", dated 2026-10-17; Thompson Hine, "FMC Issues Final Rule on Billing Requirements and Practices for Detention and Demurrage Invoices", https://www.thompsonhine.com/insights/fmc-issues-final-rule-on-billing-requirements-and-practices-for-detention-and-demurrage-invoices/, accessed 2026-10-17.

What is not proven yet

  • Willingness to pay. Whether shippers will pay to have accessorial review taken off AP's desk is the main question the pilots must answer. The lead pilot measure is AP time per invoice, before and with the product; we claim no figure until it is measured. (Business plan, section 1.)
  • Acquisition cost. The base case assumes far cheaper customer acquisition than published benchmarks suggest. The plan shows how much more money it would need at the benchmark cost instead of hiding the gap. (Business plan, sections 1 and 7; financial package, document 03.)
  • Everything forward-looking is a plan output. Every forward figure in the documents comes from the financial model or from arithmetic on stated research inputs, and each one points at the file it came from.

The funding request and its conditions are set out in the business plan (document 01) and the financial package (document 03).

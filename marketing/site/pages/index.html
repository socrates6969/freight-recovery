The problem: checking accessorials takes the time

Accessorial and detention lines arrive on carrier and broker invoices with little to check them against, so someone in AP has to find the rate confirmation, match each line and chase the ones that do not fit. In a survey by RXO, a freight broker, 61% of shippers agreed that managing accessorial requests is one of the biggest time-wasters for their accounting team. The same survey found that 70% of shippers think carriers are usually honest when requesting accessorials. So the job is mostly checking, quickly and with the evidence kept, and showing the few lines the rate confirmation does not support.

Source: RXO (a freight broker; survey of 750 shippers and carriers), "What Shippers & Carriers Really Think About Trucking Accessorials (Original Research)", 25 November 2024, https://rxo.com/resources/research/freight-accessorials/, accessed 2026-10-17.

For importers: check every D&D invoice against its required dispute window

We surface invoice-content and timing fields under 46 CFR Part 541, along with the carrier tariff, for human review. Section 541.8 gives the billed party at least 30 calendar days from invoice issuance to request mitigation, refund or waiver. Section 541.4 is currently reserved following a court decision. Deadlines and requirements depend on the billing party and facts of the invoice; this tool does not decide whether a charge is owed. Confirm current rules and any dispute with counsel or your customs broker.

For scale only: the FMC reports that the nine largest ocean carriers collected roughly $15.4 billion in D&D charges between April 2020 and March 2025. That is what was billed, not an amount anyone overpaid.

Sources: Electronic Code of Federal Regulations, 46 CFR Part 541, current through 2026-10-15, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-46/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-541; Federal Maritime Commission, "U.S. Court of Appeals Issues Decision in Case on Demurrage and Detention Billing Practices", https://www.fmc.gov/articles/u-s-court-of-appeals-issues-decision-in-case-on-demurrage-and-detention-billing-practices/, accessed 2026-10-19; Federal Maritime Commission, Detention and Demurrage, https://www.fmc.gov/detention-and-demurrage/, accessed 2026-10-19.

What it does

  1. Bring the documents. The carrier or broker invoice with its rate confirmation and BOL or gate record; for ocean fees, the D&D invoice and the carrier's tariff.
  2. Rules apply the terms. Authorized accessorials, detention clock start, free time, rate and cap, the fuel surcharge week, and the FMC rule's invoice requirements, with every step of the arithmetic shown.
  3. Get an evidence packet and a DRAFT short-pay or dispute. Per-document SHA-256 fingerprints, confirmed findings separated from items needing review, and a letter marked DRAFT / not sent.

Four cost areas, one evidence trail

  • Accessorial charges: invoice lines checked against the rate confirmation, with charge names recognised across carriers' different wording.
  • Detention billed to you: waiting time on the invoice recomputed from the contract terms and time records.
  • Fuel surcharges: recomputed from the official weekly diesel price and your own surcharge table, so a wrong week shows up.
  • Ocean container fees: demurrage and detention invoices checked against the FMC billing rule and the carrier tariff.

All four modules are built and tested on synthetic data. Every finding goes into the same evidence packet and needs a second person's approval. See every module and how it is tested.

Who it is for

  • Shippers: transportation, logistics and AP teams paying carrier and broker invoices. Check every accessorial, detention and fuel line against the rate confirmation; ambiguous lines go to a person, never disputed automatically.
  • Importers: import and logistics managers paying container D&D invoices. Check each one against the FMC rule and the carrier tariff before paying, with the dispute deadline shown.
  • Carriers, later and through partners: the same engine builds a carrier's detention packet. We plan to reach carriers through factoring and broker partners once shipper pilots exist.

What we do not do

  • We never send anything without human approval.
  • Only confirmed findings count toward a short-pay or dispute; low-confidence items are listed separately.
  • We do not move money, and we do not give legal advice.

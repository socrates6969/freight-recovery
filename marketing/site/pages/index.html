Free, no account, runs in your browser

Check freight invoices in your browser. Five agents, every line, nothing uploaded.

scoup.ai splits a freight audit into five agents with one job each: read the invoice, find the agreed terms, re-rate every line against the rate confirmation or the ocean tariff, build the evidence and check every flag. The checks run on your device: nothing you enter is uploaded, stored or sent to anyone.

The problem

Freight invoices drift from what was agreed

A rate is agreed on the rate confirmation; the invoice arrives later with a higher linehaul, an accessorial nobody authorized, or detention billed for more hours than the gate record shows. Checking every line by hand against the paperwork takes longer than most teams have, so many invoices are paid as billed.

  • $15.1B

    cost of truck driver detention to the US trucking industry in 2023: $3.6B in direct expenses and $11.5B in lost productivity (ATRI). This is the cost of waiting, mostly lost time, not money anyone can claim back.

  • 39.3%

    of stops in 2023 where drivers reported being detained (ATRI)

  • <50%

    of detention invoices paid, although 94.5% of fleets charge detention fees (ATRI). Disputes on both sides need evidence.

Audit vendors publish invoice error rates, but they sell audits and rarely show their method, so we do not quote them. scoup.ai publishes no figure for how much it finds, because none has been measured on real customer invoices.

Audit and recovery

How an overcharge turns back into money

The agents find and document; a person decides and sends. The money comes back through the ordinary billing process, not through us: we never touch payments.

  1. Check every line. The agents compare each invoice line with the rate confirmation, the tariff and the gate times, and split confirmed overcharges from items that need judgement.
  2. Build the case. Each finding gets its arithmetic, the governing term and the invoice values it rests on, in a packet you can check by hand, plus a DRAFT dispute letter.
  3. You approve and send. Nothing leaves without a person. You send the dispute to the carrier or broker, or pay only the undisputed amount where your contract allows it.
  4. The invoice is corrected. The carrier issues a corrected invoice, a credit or a refund. For ocean D&D the billed party has at least 30 days from issuance to request mitigation, refund or waiver; confirm with counsel or your customs broker.

Why agents

Why split an audit into agents

A single model asked to "audit this invoice" mixes reading, arithmetic and judgement, and fails in ways that are hard to see. Separate agents with fixed hand-offs make each step small enough to test and every result traceable.

  • One job, one test suite

    Each agent has a defined input and output, so it can be tested on its own and replaced without touching the others.

  • Code decides every amount

    Language models read documents and draft text. Amounts and entitlement come from fixed rules with exact decimal arithmetic that you can redo by hand.

  • No source, no claim

    The reviewer, in development, is built apart from the drafter. A flag that cannot be traced to an invoice line and a contract term is not reported.

  • A person approves

    Findings are drafts. Items that need judgement are kept apart and never totalled, and nothing is sent by the software.

  • Every line, not a sample

    Checks that run in seconds on your device can look at every line of every invoice instead of a sample picked by hand.

  • Runs where the data is

    In the browser app the agents that exist run on your device; once the page loads, its content security policy blocks every network connection.

Privacy by design

The app is a static bundle served from this site. Once loaded, its content security policy forbids every network connection, so it cannot send what you enter anywhere. It sets no cookies, uses no browser storage and includes no analytics. Downloads are built on your device from the values on screen. See security and privacy for the details and the limits.

What it does not do

  • It does not decide whether a charge is owed, and it does not give legal advice. Contracts and facts differ; confirm with counsel or your customs broker.
  • It does not send, short-pay or dispute anything. Letters are drafts for a person to approve.
  • It publishes no accuracy figures, because none have been measured on real customer documents.
  • It does not read PDFs or connect to a TMS, ERP or accounting system in the browser. Those belong to the team workspace, which is a prototype and not offered yet.
What the team workspace does, feature by feature

Ocean D&D invoices and the FMC rule

46 CFR Part 541 sets the information a demurrage or detention invoice must contain, requires issuance within 30 calendar days of the charge last incurred, and gives the billed party at least 30 calendar days from issuance to request mitigation, refund or waiver (section 541.8). Section 541.4 is reserved following a court decision. The audit surfaces these fields and dates for review; deadlines and requirements depend on the billing party and the facts, so confirm current rules and any dispute with counsel or your customs broker.

Questions

Is it free?

Yes. The browser tools are free to use, with no account and no sign-up. A hosted team workspace is a prototype and is not offered yet.

Where does my data go?

Nowhere. The app runs in your browser, makes no network requests after it loads, sets no cookies and keeps nothing after you close the page. Downloads are created on your device.

What are the agents?

Five stages with one job each: intake, contract and tariff lookup, re-rating and audit checks, evidence and claim drafting, and a reviewer. The checks, the arithmetic and the packet run today in the browser app as fixed rules; reading documents and the independent reviewer are in development. The Agents page goes through them step by step.

What does it check?

Accessorial and detention lines on carrier and broker invoices against the rate confirmation; ocean demurrage and detention invoices against 46 CFR Part 541 and public carrier tariffs; and charge names across carriers. Every finding shows its arithmetic. The Product page lists each rule.

Does it tell me what I owe?

No. It applies fixed, published rules to the figures you enter and lists findings for a person to review. Whether a charge is owed depends on your contract and the facts; confirm with counsel or your customs broker.

How does the money come back?

You send the dispute, not the app. The carrier then corrects the invoice, issues a credit or refunds, or you pay only the undisputed amount where your contract allows it. The app drafts the letter and the evidence; a person decides and sends.

Is this legal advice?

No. The rules, sources and arithmetic are shown so you and your counsel can decide.

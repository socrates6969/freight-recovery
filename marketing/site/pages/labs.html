scoup Labs, research notes

Measured learning, not promises

scoup Labs is where we measure whether the learning parts of scoup.ai improve with data, publish the curve and the method, and say plainly what failed and what has not been measured. Everything on this page was measured on public tariff charge names with a held-out test, not on invoices.

What Recall is

Live in the browser app

Recall is the charge-name suggester behind the Charge names tool and the workspace's charge intelligence. It is memory-based: it keeps labelled charge names as examples, turns a name into character and word fragments, and suggests the class of the nearest example when the similarity clears a fixed threshold. Below the threshold it abstains. There is no training step and there are no model weights; the memory is 256 public tariff charge names from 8 public sources and, in the workspace, a team's own labels.

It learns in two ways. Every public tariff we add gives it more examples to match against. In the workspace prototype, a label a team gives a charge description becomes an example for that team alone; labels are never shared between workspaces.

Recall is review-only. A suggestion adds an item for a person to check; it never changes an amount, confirms a charge or decides what is owed.

The learning curves

Training names in, held-out names recognised out. Every point is the same test: one public source's charge names are held out and recognised only from the other sources' names, with an exact copy of the query never allowed to count. The first run (2026-10-19) used the 175-name seed from six carriers; the second (2026-10-20) the shipped 256-name seed from 8 sources. Hover over a point, or select one, for its figures.

Keyword rules alone recognise 19.4% of the seed-v1 held-out names. Wrong suggestions stayed between 0.6% and 2.5% in the seed-v1 run and between 1.2% and 0.4% in the seed-v2 run. The curve is flattening: in the seed-v2 run each doubling of the training names added +21.7, +13.3 and +8.2 points. Measured on public tariff charge names held out by source; not on invoices.

More curves from the same runs

  • Similarity threshold

    Wrong suggestions stay at one item across thresholds from 0.20 to 0.50. A lower threshold looks better on the same held-out names, but when the operating point is chosen per fold from the other folds it does not beat 0.30, so 0.30 stays.

  • Per category

    Detention names go from 65% recognised at 10% of the training names to 100% at all of them; fuel reaches 75% of its names at 100%; accessorial names track the pooled curve; linehaul has one name, so there is nothing to match.

  • A team's own labels

    Adding up to 20 of a carrier's own labels changed recognition by +0.9 and +0.6 points in the two runs, not significant (p = 1): no measurable effect on tariff names, which the other sources' public names already cover.

  • One candidate not shipped

    Character 2-4-grams plus words at the fixed threshold gained +4.6 and +5.1 points with no new wrong suggestions. Held back until it can be measured on invoice lines, where the extra matches could go either way.

  • The outlook stays at its ceiling

    Beyond eight years of price history the cost outlook barely moves: the error ratio goes from 0.882 to 0.873 (diesel, one week ahead) and from 0.846 to 0.842 (PPI, one month ahead) at 22 to 24 years.

Three results from the two runs

81 more public names: 89.1% to 93.1%

On 2026-10-19 we fetched 99 charge names from three further public sources; after source verification 81 were kept (11 J.B. Hunt names dropped because the page forbids reproduction, 7 GSA rule-only items because their text states no charge). The shipped seed is now 256 names from 8 public sources. On the unchanged 175 held-out names it recognises 163, 93.1%, with 1 wrong suggestion; the earlier run with all 99 names gave the same result: 7 names gained, 0 lost, exact McNemar p = 0.016. The added names were labelled by one person and checked, not blind, by a second.

Two candidates failed and were not adopted

Every candidate is written down before it runs, with the held-out test and the pass mark it must reach. An IDF reweighting of the same features, meant to discount generic words such as "charge" and "fee", fell from 89.1% to 76.6% recognised (0 names gained, 22 lost): with the threshold fixed in advance, fewer names cleared it. A vote that added a sentence-embedding model next to the character match did not pass its gate either. Neither shipped. The product changes only when a candidate beats the incumbent on the same held-out names, and what fails is reported, not quietly dropped.

The cost outlook is at its ceiling on public data

The workspace's cost outlook fits an AR(2) model to public price history: the weekly U.S. diesel price from EIA and the monthly truckload PPI from BLS, both via FRED. Over every forecast origin of the last ten years, its error is 0.882 times that of a no-change forecast for diesel one week ahead and 0.846 for PPI one month ahead. Fitting on more than eight years of history changes these by less than a point: more public history will not move it, and a lane-level outlook would need other data.

What it means when you use scoup.ai

A charge under a name your rules have never seen is the one most likely to slip through. Recall's job is to route it to a person: when an unfamiliar name resembles a labelled one closely enough, the line is marked for review with the name it resembles and the similarity score. When it does not, Recall says nothing and the fixed rules still apply.

A suggestion never changes an amount, never confirms a charge and never decides what is owed. The arithmetic in every finding comes from the fixed rules, and a person approves every claim.

What is not known

  • How Recall performs on customer invoice lines. Tariff names are clean; invoice text carries carrier codes, abbreviations and typos, a different distribution. Nothing has been measured on it.
  • Nothing has been measured on customer documents of any kind, and no accuracy figure is published for invoices.
  • The results on this page come from two internal runs by the same team and have not been independently reproduced.
  • One wrong suggestion is 0.4 to 0.6 points, and the small classes (fuel 4 names, linehaul 1) move by 25 points or more per name.

What is next, and what it needs

Each item carries a label. Planned means it can be done with public data and is scheduled. Needs customer data means it cannot be measured until pilot teams share files or outcomes under NDA, and its gain is unknown until then.

  • More public tariff names

    Planned

    Further public carrier tariffs, each label checked by a second person before it ships, measured on the same held-out test. The curve is flattening: the last doubling added +8.2 points, the 81 added names +4.0 on the first held-out set, and 4 of the 7 names gained were the same storage concept, so public names alone will not settle how Recall does on invoices.

  • Recall on real invoice lines

    Needs customer data

    Labelled invoice lines from pilot files, shared under NDA. Carrier codes, abbreviations and free text are a different distribution from tariff names, and nothing has been measured on them. This is the measurement that decides whether the public curve carries over, and whether the 2-4-gram candidate ships.

  • Dispute outcomes feeding suggestion precision

    Needs customer data

    Today nothing is recorded after a demand is marked send-ready, so no one can say how many review items became captured dollars. With outcomes (paid, denied, partial) recorded per finding, the precision of each suggestion rule can be measured and the rules tuned against it.

  • A learned review-priority ranker

    Needs customer data

    The claim list is ordered by a stated policy with fixed weights. A ranker fitted on outcomes would be compared with that policy on captured dollars per hour of review, on held-out claims, and adopted only if it wins.

  • Lane-level cost forecasting

    Needs customer data

    The public outlook is national and at its ceiling. A lane-level forecast needs a customer's lane rate history or purchased benchmarks, and it ships only if it beats a seasonal baseline on held-out months.

  • Agent-orchestration research

    Tooling, not in the product

    The neural mesh of coding agents and the Hebbwise learner we also write about are tooling for building scoup.ai. No agent or learned router runs in the browser app or the workspace, and no model decides an amount.

Method and sources

Held-out test: leave-one-source-out over the public tariff charge names in the seed. Each source's names are held out in turn and classified only from the other sources' names; a candidate whose normalised text equals the query is excluded, so an exact copy can never count as recognised. A name counts as recognised when the nearest example's class is correct and the similarity clears the fixed threshold; below it, Recall abstains. For the smaller training sizes the other sources' names are randomly subsampled 50 times and the rates averaged. The seed-v1 run uses the 175 names from six carriers (159 of them accessorial names, so the test says most about that class); the seed-v2 run uses the shipped 256 names from 8 sources (234 accessorial, 17 detention, 4 fuel, 1 linehaul), with the GSA tariff as one source.

Runs of 2026-10-19 and 2026-10-20 by the scoup.ai team on public data: charge names copied from six public carrier tariffs (retrieved 2026-10-11) plus 81 names kept from the GSA Uniform Rules Tariff 200-A and the FedEx Freight 2026 accessorial sheet (retrieved 2026-10-19); diesel and PPI series from FRED (EIA series GASDESW, BLS series PCU4841214841212). The curve is produced by a script in the repository, packages/shared/src/charge-intel/curve.ts with the command npm run eval:curve (api/src/eval/curve.ts, flags --seed v1|v2), which reproduces the 2026-10-19 run to two decimals. These are internal results on public tariff charge names, not a benchmark on invoices, and they have not been independently reproduced.
